# Code of conduct

This is a small project that wants honest technical conversation. These rules keep it that way.

## What we expect
- **Be specific and be kind.** Criticise code, claims and ideas, not people. "This check is wrong because..." is welcome; "this is stupid" is not.
- **Say what you actually know.** Mark guesses as guesses. The project itself separates what was verified from what was assumed, and so should discussions.
- **Assume good faith**, and when you disagree, say what evidence would change your mind.
- **Respect privacy.** Never post secrets, customer data or other people's personal data in issues, pull requests or examples. Redact first.

## What is not acceptable
- Harassment, insults, threats, discrimination, or unwelcome personal attention.
- Posting someone's private information.
- Spam, self-promotion unrelated to the project, and attempts to buy, trade or fake stars, forks or reviews.
- Pushing the project to claim something it cannot prove (for example "certified" or "compliant"). A pull request that adds such a claim will be declined.

## Enforcement
The maintainer may edit, hide or remove comments, close issues and pull requests, and block accounts that break these rules. Decisions are explained when that is safe to do.

## Reporting
- For something happening in a public thread, use GitHub's **Report content** option on it.
- To report privately, open a **private security advisory** (Security tab, "Report a vulnerability") and put `conduct` in the title. It reaches only the maintainer.
- A first response is usually given within a week.

This document is written in our own words; it follows the spirit of widely used community codes of conduct.
